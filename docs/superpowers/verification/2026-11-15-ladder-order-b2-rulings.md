## The maintainer's own decisions

The design's §2 holds three decisions of the maintainer's for this batch, dated 2026-11-14: *The maintainer's, 2026-11-14. Each was put as a question with the alternatives sketched, and M2 follows from the choice made at M1.* The ledger cites them on its line 8, beside one instruction of 2026-11-15.

| Decision | What was decided |
|---|---|
| **M1** | **A command stops when the document it builds from was never produced.** Not every rung: `/idea`, an ARD on a single-component PRD, Epics, and the PRD-level specification `/epics` reads stay optional, and on the BRD route no `prd.md` is required. The alternative refused was *every rung of the ladder*. |
| **M2** | **It stops; it does not ask.** The alternative refused was a *Stop (Recommended) / Go on without it* question. |
| **M3** | **A keyed `/implement` needs both a specification and a design**, on every run for the unit, a later run included. A unit built earlier without a design stops when a follow-up is run for its key; the maintainer accepted that cost. A direct `/implement`, with no key, stays the way to make a small change with no documents. The alternatives refused were *a specification is enough* and *both on a first run only*. |
| *Go batch to batch without asking* (2026-11-15) | An instruction about the run, not a decision on its content (line 8). The controller ruled in the maintainer's place throughout; the ledger records no question put to the maintainer during the run. |

Rulings 3, 9 and 14 read M3's *every run for the unit* as every **fresh** keyed run for the unit: a resumed run is not stopped by the four new stops. Ruling 9 gives the reason, the design's sentence was annotated (commit `97078420`), and the ledger records no question to the maintainer on it. The final review judged it sound and found no way for a fresh run to become a resume.

The ledger names the run's models (line 22: Opus implementers and task reviewers, Fable for the final whole-branch review, Sonnet for narrow re-reviews of small fix diffs) and does not say who chose them. Rulings 37, 39, 40 and 42 rest on one standing rule of the maintainer's, set before this run: no known defect ships — in ruling 40's words, a ruled-in fix is not shipped as a note, and in ruling 42's, a bug found in passing is fixed before push. Ruling 41 rests on another: an Opus review of every change before its pull request, however small.
